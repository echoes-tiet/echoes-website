---
title: "Scientists discover Protein that Reverses Brain Aging"
author: "Palak Jindal"
cover: "/images/bulletin/20febs.png"
order: 12
---

<p>As we grow older, the brain gradually loses some of its ability to produce new neurons. One major reason
is that <strong>neural stem cells</strong>, which generate new brain cells, become less active over time.
For many years, scientists believed this decline was an irreversible consequence of aging DNA. However,
new research from the <strong>National University of Singapore</strong> suggests that the process may be
more adaptable than previously thought.</p>
<p>The study, published in <strong>Science Advances</strong>, focuses on a protein called <b>DMTF1</b>. In
younger brains, this protein is present at higher levels and helps maintain stem cell activity. As people
age, <b>DMTF1</b> levels decrease, causing these cells to become dormant. This reduced activity is
associated with memory decline and slower cognitive function.</p>
<p>In laboratory experiments using aged human cells and mouse models, researchers increased <b>DMTF1</b>
levels. The older stem cells then began functioning more like younger ones. The protein appears to relax
tightly packed DNA, allowing certain genes to reactivate and support cell division. As a result, the
production of new neurons improved.</p>
<p>Interestingly, the effect was observed even when <strong>telomeres</strong>, often described as a cell’s
biological clock, were shortened. This suggests that restoring stem cell function may not require fully
repairing aging DNA.</p>
<p>Although the research is still in its early stages and has not yet led to human treatments, it provides
valuable insight into how brain aging works and points toward possible future strategies for supporting
cognitive health. While the results are promising, translating this discovery into safe human treatments
could take several years, as further animal studies and multiple phases of clinical trials will be
required.</p>
