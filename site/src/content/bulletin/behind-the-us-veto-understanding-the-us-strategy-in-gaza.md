<p>On <b>18 September 2025</b>, the <strong>United States</strong> cast the sole vote to block a <strong>UN
Security Council</strong> draft that would have demanded an “immediate, unconditional and permanent
ceasefire” in <strong>Gaza</strong>. Fourteen members supported the text, drafted by ten elected members
(<b>Algeria, Denmark, Greece, Guyana, Pakistan, Panama, Republic of Korea, Sierra Leone, Slovenia, and
Somalia</b>), while the US opposed. As U.S. deputy envoy <strong>Morgan Ortagus</strong> put it before
the vote: “It fails to condemn Hamas or recognise Israel’s right to defend itself, and it wrongly
legitimises the false narratives benefitting Hamas, which have sadly found currency in this council.”</p>

<p>The draft characterised conditions in <strong>Gaza</strong> as “catastrophic,” demanded the release of
hostages and called for lifting restrictions on aid. The conflict traces to the <b>7 October 2023</b>
Hamas-led attack on southern <strong>Israel</strong> (≈<b>1,200</b> killed; ~<b>250</b> taken hostage);
Gaza’s Hamas-run health authorities report more than <b>65,000</b> deaths and mounting malnutrition and
displacement since. Sponsors and UN officials stressed the Council’s majority message for relief and
hostage resolution; Palestinian Ambassador <strong>Riyad Mansour</strong> called the US action “deeply
regrettable,” saying the veto prevented the Council from acting to protect civilians.</p>

<p>Implications are immediate and structural: the veto sustains diplomatic paralysis at the <strong>Security
Council</strong>, shifts pressure to the <strong>General Assembly</strong> and other fora, and
intensifies NGO and UN appeals for open crossings and scaled humanitarian access. Humanitarian agencies
(<strong>MSF</strong>, <strong>IRC</strong>) described the veto as a grave setback to relief efforts and
urged alternative diplomatic tracks to secure aid and hostage releases.</p>
