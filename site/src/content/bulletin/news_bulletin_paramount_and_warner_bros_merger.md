# When Giants Collide: Paramount and Warner Bros. Rewrite Hollywood

**Category:** Media & Entertainment  

---

## Overview

Hollywood has a new power centre. On October 6, Paramount Skydance, led by David Ellison, completed its takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery, and the combined company now operates as **Skydance**. Two of the industry’s oldest studios, two streaming services and two newsrooms now sit under one roof, in one of the biggest shake-ups in the history of American entertainment.

## Content Library & Portfolio

The combined library brings together an unprecedented array of intellectual property:

| Category | Key Assets & Franchises |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Franchises & IPs** | *Harry Potter*, *Game of Thrones*, *DC*, *Looney Tunes*, *Top Gun*, *Star Trek*, *Yellowstone*, *SpongeBob SquarePants* |
| **Film Archives** | *The Godfather*, *Casablanca*, Miramax catalog |
| **Networks & Services** | HBO, CBS, Nickelodeon, Paramount+, HBO Max |

Getting here was a drama of its own. Netflix pulled out of the bidding war, Paramount stepped in, and legal challenges from state attorneys general and the Writers Guild were settled before the deal could close.

## Industry Impact & Future Strategy

Now comes the part audiences will actually notice:
* **Production Commitments:** Skydance has announced production commitments of at least 30 theatrical films a year, each with a minimum 45-day theatrical window.
* **Television Output:** More than 180 television shows and series are planned, establishing a crowded release calendar for cinemas and streaming platforms.
* **Financial Strategy:** The company is targeting at least \$6 billion in cost savings within three years, requiring a balance between blockbuster ambition and operational efficiency.
* **Streaming Integration:** Paramount+ and HBO Max are expected to merge eventually into one service to simplify user navigation. Ellison has stated that HBO will retain its distinct brand identity.

Hollywood has seen studio mergers before, but rarely one that brings together this many franchises, channels and storytellers. Whether Skydance uses this scale to take creative risks or play it safe with sequels and spin-offs remains the core question facing the industry.
