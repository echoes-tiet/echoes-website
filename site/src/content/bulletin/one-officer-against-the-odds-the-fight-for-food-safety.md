<p>
From crowded street corners to packed neighbourhood eateries, food is everywhere and so is the
<strong>trust</strong> people place in those who prepare and serve it. Every day, millions of people eat
at roadside stalls, restaurants, bakeries, and dairies, and increasingly order through
<strong>quick-commerce platforms</strong>, trusting that what reaches their plate is safe. Yet that
trust comes with a blind spot. A study of urban Indian consumers found that while around
<strong>90% reported reading food labels</strong>, most primarily checked manufacturing or expiry dates,
with only about a third checking <em>ingredients or nutrition information</em>.
</p>

<p>
<strong>The Crackdown in Maharashtra:</strong> Since IAS officer <strong>Tukaram Mundhe</strong> took
charge as Commissioner of the <strong>Maharashtra Food and Drug Administration (FDA)</strong>, the
department has pursued an unusually visible inspection drive across the state. More than
<strong>3,000 establishments</strong> have been inspected, with <strong>165 licences suspended</strong>,
alongside hundreds of improvement notices and the seizure or destruction of large quantities of
unfit food products. Quick-commerce facilities, dark stores, dairies, and restaurants have faced
rigorous scrutiny, uncovering issues like <em>fly infestations, dirty drains, improper storage, and unclean
surfaces</em>.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Uncovering the Invisible Hazards:</strong> These inspections expose how much of food safety
remains <em>invisible</em> to the everyday consumer. A customer cannot see whether a kitchen is
maintaining refrigeration temperatures, storing raw and cooked food separately, adulterating milk, or
handling ingredients unhygienically. The FDA has actively targeted the dairy sector alongside strict
crackdowns on the <span>excessive use of artificial food colours</span> and <strong>reused cooking
oil</strong>.
</p>

<p>
<strong>A Measurable Shift in Behaviour:</strong> The clearest sign of the crackdown is what has
changed outside the inspection reports. Food vendors have reportedly begun reducing or eliminating
<em>synthetic dyes</em> in favor of natural alternatives, improving <strong>cooking-oil
practices</strong>, and adopting gloves, aprons, and head coverings. Even the familiar, artificially
saturated colours of popular street foods are fading away—reflecting a larger shift toward
<span>hygiene consciousness and accountability</span> across the food industry.
</p>

<p>
<strong>Summary:</strong> Maharashtra’s intensified food-safety crackdown under FDA Commissioner
<strong>Tukaram Mundhe</strong> has brought restaurants, dairies, food outlets, and quick-commerce
facilities under unprecedented scrutiny. With over <em>3,000 raids conducted</em> and hundreds facing
regulatory action, the drive is driving tangible behavioural changes in kitchen sanitation and
ingredient safety, sparking an essential nationwide conversation on everyday food standards.
</p>
