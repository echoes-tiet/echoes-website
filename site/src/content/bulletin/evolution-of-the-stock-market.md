<p>
The <strong>Indian stock market</strong> has experienced a remarkable transformation over the past few decades. From traditional trading methods to instant digital transactions, the market has evolved alongside India's economy. The <strong>BSE Sensex</strong> and <strong>Nifty 50</strong> have become two of the most recognised indicators of this development.
</p>

<p>
One of the early milestones came in <strong>1990</strong>, when the <strong>Sensex crossed 1,000 points</strong>. Soon after, <strong>economic reforms in the 1990s</strong> opened the Indian economy to greater investment and competition. Another major change came with the establishment of the <strong>National Stock Exchange (NSE)</strong>. Its introduction of <strong>screen-based trading</strong> helped make the market faster and more organised. The <strong>Nifty 50</strong> was launched in <strong>1996</strong>, providing a benchmark representing 50 major companies from different sectors.
</p>

<p>
The next decade brought rapid growth. The <strong>Sensex crossed 10,000 points in 2006</strong> and reached <strong>20,000 in 2007</strong>. However, the <strong>global financial crisis of 2008</strong> caused a sharp decline in markets across the world, including India. This period showed that stock-market growth is accompanied by uncertainty and that even record highs can be followed by significant falls.
</p>

<p>
The market gradually recovered and entered another period of expansion. The <strong>Sensex crossed 30,000 in 2015</strong> and <strong>40,000 in 2019</strong>. At the same time, technological developments changed the way people invested. <strong>Online trading, dematerialised shares and mobile investment applications</strong> made the market increasingly accessible to individual investors.
</p>

<p>
The <strong>COVID-19 pandemic</strong> created another major turning point. Indian markets experienced a sharp fall during the early months of <strong>2020</strong> as uncertainty spread globally. However, the recovery was strong. The <strong>Sensex crossed 50,000 in January 2021</strong> and later moved beyond <strong>60,000</strong>, reflecting renewed investor confidence.
</p>

<p>
The upward movement continued in the following years. The <strong>Nifty crossed 20,000 in 2023</strong>, followed by another major milestone in <strong>2024</strong>, when it crossed <strong>25,000</strong>. The <strong>Sensex also crossed 80,000 for the first time in July 2024</strong>. These levels demonstrated the increasing size and importance of India's equity market.
</p>

<p>
The story did not stop at the records of 2024. In <strong>2025</strong>, the <strong>Nifty 50 gained around 10.5%</strong> over the year, ending at approximately <strong>26,130</strong>.
</p>

<p>
The year <strong>2026</strong> added another milestone as the <strong>Nifty 50 completed 30 years since its launch</strong>. NSE also reported that its <strong>registered investor base crossed 13 crore</strong>, highlighting the growing participation of Indians in the capital market.
</p>

<p>
The journey of the Indian stock market is therefore not simply a story of rising numbers. It reflects <strong>economic reforms, technological progress, business expansion and increasing public participation</strong>. From <strong>1,000 points to today's much larger market</strong>, each milestone represents another stage in India's financial development. As the country continues to grow, the stock market is likely to remain an important part of its economic story.
</p>
