<p>
              <strong>BRICS</strong> is a pivotal <strong>intergovernmental alliance</strong> comprising major
              <em>emerging economies</em> that serves as a
              <span>political and diplomatic coordination forum</span> for the <strong>Global South</strong>.
              Representing approximately
              <strong>49.5% of the global population</strong> and nearly <strong>40% of global GDP</strong>, the bloc
              acts as a
              <em>critical counterweight</em> to Western-dominated institutions like the <strong>World Bank</strong> and
              the
              <strong>IMF</strong>. For <strong>India</strong>, BRICS is a <em>strategic necessity</em> for maintaining
              a
              <strong>multipolar world order</strong>, balancing the <span>Russia-China axis</span>, and accessing
              <strong>developmental financing</strong> through the <strong>New Development Bank</strong>, which has
              already funded
              <strong>18 projects in India</strong> totalling <strong>USD 6.9 billion</strong>.
            </p>

            <p>
              On <strong>13 January 2026</strong>, <strong>India</strong> reached a <em>significant milestone</em> in
              its
              <strong>diplomatic calendar</strong> by officially unveiling the <strong>logo and website</strong> for its
              <strong>BRICS 2026 Presidency</strong>. During a <span>high-profile ceremony</span> in <strong>New
                Delhi</strong>,
              <strong>External Affairs Minister Dr. S. Jaishankar</strong> launched the presidency, marking the
              grouping’s
              <strong>20th anniversary</strong> since its inception. The event showcased India’s
              <em>people-centric vision</em> for the alliance under the theme,
              <strong>“Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability”</strong>.
            </p>

            <p>
              The newly unveiled <strong>logo</strong> is <em>deeply symbolic</em>, drawing inspiration from the
              <strong>lotus</strong>, India’s <span>national flower</span>, to represent
              <strong>collective strength and unity</strong>. Its design features
              <em>inner petals forming a “Namaste” gesture</em>, signifying
              <span>warmth, respect, and harmonious collaboration</span>. Furthermore,
              <strong>five petals</strong> are specifically coloured to represent the
              <strong>founding members</strong>—<strong>Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South
                Africa</strong>—highlighting the
              group’s <em>inclusive nature</em>. Alongside the logo, the official website
              <strong>(brics2026.gov.in)</strong> was launched to serve as a
              <span>common platform</span> for disseminating information on
              <em>upcoming meetings and strategic initiatives</em>. This launch signals
              India’s <strong>readiness to lead</strong> the <strong>expanded ten-nation bloc</strong> toward
              <em>enhanced global welfare and resilience</em>.
            </p>
