<p>
              As we move into early <strong>2026</strong>, the launch of the <strong>Noida International Airport
                (NIA)</strong> in <strong>Jewar</strong> is officially more than just an infrastructure milestone—it is
              a <em>massive win for sustainability</em>.
              NIA has just secured the <strong>IGBC Green Campus certification</strong>, making it the first
              project of this scale in India to be recognized for its entire <strong>1,334-hectare
                ecosystem</strong>.
              Developed as a partnership between the <strong>UP government</strong> and <strong>Zurich Airport
                International</strong>, this is not just a place to catch a flight; it is a living <em>"Net-Zero"</em>
              case study.
              Here is a breakdown of why this matters for the <strong>future of Indian aviation</strong>:
            </p>

            <p>
              <strong>A Massive Solar Footprint:</strong> The airport is anchored by an <strong>82.94-acre solar
                farm</strong> with a generation capacity of over
              <strong>51,000 megawatt-hours</strong>, enabling operations to run primarily on <em>renewable energy</em>.
              This clean power backbone effectively <span>decouples airport growth from the conventional power
                grid</span> and
              <em>significantly reduces carbon emissions</em> from the outset, positioning Jewar Airport as a
              <strong>low-carbon aviation hub</strong>.
            </p>

            <p>
              <strong>100% Electric Airside Operations:</strong> In a first for Indian airports, the entire airside
              where aircraft park, taxi, and are serviced will operate on
              <strong>electric mobility</strong>. All baggage tugs, passenger shuttles, and ground service vehicles are
              <em>battery-powered</em>, supported by a <span>dense network of charging stations</span> embedded across
              the tarmac, ensuring
              <strong>efficient, emission-free ground operations</strong>.
            </p>

            <p>
              <strong>Rethinking the Commute:</strong> To promote <em>sustainable passenger mobility</em>,
              <strong>20% of the airport’s parking capacity</strong> is reserved for <strong>electric vehicles</strong>
              and equipped with
              <em>standard and fast-charging facilities</em>. Future-ready plans such as <span>pod taxis</span> and a
              <strong>dedicated rapid rail–metro corridor</strong> aim to reduce reliance on <em>private vehicles</em>,
              making
              <strong>low-emission public transport</strong> the preferred mode of access to the airport.
            </p>

            <p>
              <strong>Circular Waste &amp; Water Neutrality:</strong> The Jewar Airport campus has been designed as a
              <em>self-sustaining ecosystem</em> with a strong emphasis on <strong>circular resource
                management</strong>.
              It incorporates <span>two large rainwater harvesting ponds</span> to capture and store runoff,
              significantly reducing
              dependence on <em>external water sources</em>. A <strong>zero-liquid discharge Sewage Treatment Plant
                (STP)</strong> ensures that
              all wastewater generated on campus is <em>treated and reused</em>. Additionally, an on-site
              <strong>Renewable Natural Gas (RNG) plant</strong> converts <span>organic waste</span> into <em>clean
                fuel</em> for ground
              vehicle and emergency backup systems. Together, these systems establish a <strong>closed-loop
                model</strong> for water
              and waste management, reinforcing the airport’s commitment to <em>water neutrality, waste minimization,
                and long-term environmental resilience</em>.
            </p>
